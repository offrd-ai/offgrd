# SpiralXO vs OFFOPS (ODK Labs) — competitive analysis + build plan

**Researched:** July 2026 · spiralxo.com, spiralxo.com/lite, CoachFore feature (Mar 2026), App Store / Google Play listings.
**Bottom line up front:** SpiralXO is the closest thing to a direct competitor we've seen. They are **ahead of us on distribution, integration, and onboarding** — not on coaching depth. Their wedge is *consolidation* ("replace 9 tools with 1"). Ours is *measurement* ("prove the practice worked"). We should not try to out-breadth them. We should make the loop they don't have impossible to ignore, and close three specific gaps that are currently blocking adoption.

## 2. How they position themselves

Their homepage leads with consolidation, not coaching: *"Playbook, game prep, player learning, in-game analytics, film breakdown, and communication — one system built for football. Stop stitching five tools together to do what one should."* The centerpiece of the page is a teardown table — **"Most Programs Setup: 9+ separate tools" vs "With SpiralXO: 1 platform"** — that names competitors directly: Hudl, PQD, Google Forms, Remind, Band, group texts, WordPress, Notepad, Google Sheets, Excel, QwikCut. That's a procurement argument aimed at the head coach and AD: fewer subscriptions, fewer logins, fewer contracts.

The secondary theme is **time reclaimed**, aimed at the coordinator: *"Stop Managing Chaos. Start Coaching,"* *"saves you 10+ hours a week,"* and on SpiralXO Lite, *"Save 20+ hours every week."* Their testimonials reinforce it — Gallatin's DC: *"Do yourself a favor and save time on the weekends. Your family will thank you."* Note the Lite product is explicitly **offense-first** and AI-branded: *"Start Using Your AI Playcaller To Gameplan… keep your offense organized."* Credibility is carried by championship logos (Verona 16x state champion, Peach County 3x, Parkview 3x) and a sponsored CoachFore long-form feature by Chris Fore — a recognized HS coaching media figure.

## 3. What they actually do well

**1. Distribution and social proof — their biggest lead over us, and it isn't a feature.** 25+ named partner programs with championship pedigree across GA, NJ, TN, IA, WA, CO, MI, CT, CA, AZ, MD, NE, OH. Named coordinators on record. A sponsored feature on CoachFore reaching exactly our ICP. We cannot pretend this away: in a head-to-head, a coach will ask "who else runs this?" and they have an answer.

**2. Hudl/QwikCut bidirectional integration.** Import tagged Hudl data → auto-broken-down scouting report (formations, tendencies, down-and-distance, personnel); export the full game log to Hudl/QwikCut **in one click** post-game. This is the single most important integration in the category because Hudl is the incumbent workflow. It removes the #1 switching objection — "we already do our film in Hudl."

**3. Onboarding as an operational moat.** *"From Demo to Live in 24 Hours"* — they send a personalized registration link and **configure roster, teams, and settings for you**, then provide guided onboarding for the first 4 weeks plus weekly Q&A calls. For a HS staff with no IT support, done-for-you setup beats any feature list.

**4. A real PLG funnel.** Free **SpiralXO Lite** (no credit card), a live playbook demo, and a self-paced interactive tour — a land-and-expand path from free offense tool → full platform. Plus published pricing, which reduces friction for budget-constrained ADs.

**5. Genuine product breadth.** Live in-game analytics (one coach tags, whole staff sees tendencies + next-play prediction), scout-card generation tied to your terminology with a compounding library, call-sheet generation, equipment management, a **dedicated parent portal**, multi-team (Varsity/JV/9th) packaged in-price, and native mobile apps.

## 4. Where they're weak

**1. Learning is completion-shaped, not deficiency-shaped.** Their player learning tracks *"who completed it, who understood it"* via quizzes, flashcards, matching, and route-drawing scored for accuracy. That's meaningfully better than a PDF install — but it measures **knowledge recall**, not **skill deficiency at scheme resolution**, and critically it has **no re-test loop**. Nothing in their public material claims: we found the exact cell you're failing, drilled it, and proved the number moved next week.

**2. Offense-tilted teaching.** Lite is explicitly *"keep your offense organized"* / *"AI Playcaller."* Their learning primitives (route drawing, assignment quizzes) are offense-native. There's no evidence of a defensive engine that *composes* a defense — coverage rules, man-match, zone drops, motion adjustment — and grades a defender's **alignment** against it. Defensive teaching looks like a gap.

**3. Recruiting is thin — and gated.** "Public Recruiting Page and Portal" is **Elite-tier only** and reads as a website + portal, not a recruiting engine. No evidence of a CRM (contacts/follow-up/targets), college matching, or player-readiness scoring.

**4. Pricing opacity beneath the headline.** "$1,500/yr" is a *starting* price that varies by team size; the real number requires a sales call. Free-trial policy is explicitly discretionary: *"We handle trials on a case-by-case basis."*

**Evidence caveat:** I found **no independent third-party reviews** (no G2/Capterra/Reddit threads of substance). Their public proof is first-party testimonials and sponsored content. Their weaknesses above are inferred from what their marketing *doesn't* claim — a reasonable but not airtight signal.

## 5. How we compare

### Where we're clearly stronger

**1. The closed measurement loop — our actual moat.** Focus Today (Slices 1→4d + G) does something I can find no evidence of anywhere in their product: it identifies a deficiency at **scheme × dimension** resolution ("DB depth vs Cover 1 — 0/8"), names the individual it concentrates in, maps it to a drill, **scopes it to this week's install and opponent frequency**, pushes it into the practice script, flags it for re-test, **up-weights the next test generation toward that exact cell**, and then shows the week-over-week delta proving practice moved the number. Their loop ends at "he completed the install." Ours ends at "the number moved from 2/7 to 5/9."

**2. Rep-level diagnostic granularity.** Alignment reps are graded on **relationship × leverage × depth separately**, with partial credit and motion adjustment. "0/8 on depth vs Cover 1" is a coaching instrument that tells a position coach exactly what to drill on Tuesday. A quiz score of 78% is not.

**3. A defensive teaching engine, not a quiz bank.** `composeDefense` simulates the defense from the coach's own authored calls — coverage rules, man-match (Cover 0/1), zone drops per coverage, fronts/stunts, OL protection keys, motion adjustment — and grades the player against the *actual* look. This is a category harder to build than quizzes, and it's where their product looks thinnest.

**4. Recruiting depth (getOFFRD).** Real CRM (contacts, follow-up, targets, analytics), college matching, the two-score split (Game-Ready vs Recruitability), promote-players, public profile, AI GM, parent digest. Against an Elite-tier public page, this isn't close — and it's the piece that earns **player and parent** adoption, not just coach adoption.

### Where they're clearly stronger

**1. Distribution and credibility.** 25+ named champion programs vs our handful. This is the gap that actually loses deals.
**2. Hudl/QwikCut round-trip.** Table stakes we don't have. Every coach's film life already lives in Hudl.
**3. Time-to-value.** 24-hour go-live with done-for-you configuration vs our cold-start burden (a program must author defenses, def_aligns, and week plans before our best features light up).
**4. Packaging and funnel.** Free tier, published pricing, native apps, self-serve demo. We have none of these.

### Where it depends

- **Playbook design** — theirs optimizes speed-of-drawing (reuse formations/routes/schemes, animation). Ours optimizes teaching fidelity (wizard + engine + auto-seeded tests). A coach who wants to draw fast may prefer theirs; a coach who wants players *tested* on what he drew prefers ours.
- **Call sheet / game plan generation** — they market it as a headline; we have week plans + AI GM weekly package. Likely rough parity, but theirs is proven in market and ours isn't.
- **In-game analytics** — they have live tag → staff-wide real-time sync → next-play prediction. We have gameday + Scout Predict/Tendencies. Our multi-coach live sync story is unverified; assume they lead until we prove otherwise.

## 6. Positioning recommendation

**They own consolidation. We should own proof.** SpiralXO's argument is procurement — *"replace 9 tools with 1."* Ours should be coaching outcome — *"the only system that proves your practice time worked."* Consolidation is an argument you win with a purchasing manager; proof is an argument you win with a head coach who just lost a game to mental errors. Don't try to out-breadth them (equipment management is a losing battle). Make the re-test loop the headline of the product and the marketing, and close the three gaps that keep us out of the conversation: **Hudl round-trip, time-to-value, and public proof.**

## 7. Messaging do's and don'ts

**Do say**
- "Every platform tells you who *completed* the install. We tell you who's actually fixed — and prove it with the number."
- "We diagnose at the cell: *DB depth vs Cover 1, 0/8.* Not a 78% quiz score."
- "Your next test automatically drills the thing you're failing — and shows you the week-over-week delta."
- "Your defense gets taught by an engine that runs your actual calls, with motion adjustments."
- "Your players get recruiting upside, not just homework." (family/retention hook)

**Don't say**
- "We replace 9 tools." — That's their line, and today they do it better. Don't fight on their turf.
- Anything implying we integrate with Hudl until we actually do. That claim gets checked in the first demo.
- "We're easier to use." — Not yet defensible (see §8); earn it first.
- Anything dismissive of their championship logos. Acknowledge and redirect to the loop.
- Vague AI claims. They already brand "AI Playcaller"; generic AI talk reads as me-too.

## 8. The honest read on us — what to fix before we sell against them

I ran our live gameday flow this week; this is what a coach would actually hit.

| Issue | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Multi-origin auth fragility** — offops.app / getoffrd.com/gameday / staging aliases; sessions bounced between origins, one origin never held a session at all | A coach who gets signed out on a sideline is a churned coach. Their pitch is literally "stop managing software." |

| **No free tier, no published pricing, no logos** | We're absent from the evaluation before the demo starts. |

## 9. Build plan — a better and more intuitive system

### A. Sharpen the wedge (own "proof")
1. **Promote Focus Impact to the front door.** Program-level readiness trend over time, "what moved this week" as the headline of the coach hub — the delta is the product.
2. **Weekly HC digest**: "3 cells emphasized · 2 improved · 1 still stuck," auto-generated.
3. **Mastery per cell for players** — visible progress on *the thing they're bad at*, not a generic completion bar.

### B. Kill the cold start (biggest adoption blocker)
4. **Import-to-install**: Hudl/scouting import → auto-derive opponent looks → auto-seed `def_aligns` and alignment tests. A coach should get real reps **without authoring anything**.
5. **Starter systems library** — 4-3/3-4, Cover 0–4, Tampa 2, common fronts/stunts, one-tap install and editable. (Today a missing authored Cover-1 call silently produced zero reps — that's the cold start biting.)
6. **Concierge onboarding** — match their 24-hour go-live: we configure roster, teams, system. This is a service, not a feature, and it's winnable immediately.

### C. Close table stakes
7. **Hudl + QwikCut round-trip** — import tagged film; one-click game-log export. Highest-ROI integration we can build; removes the #1 objection.
8. **Native app shells** (or installable PWA done properly) + **offline sideline mode**. Stadium wifi is the real operating environment.

### D. Fix the plumbing that erodes trust
9. **One origin, one session.** Collapse the origin sprawl; make SSO boringly reliable.
10. **Unify team/school resolution** — one resolver for rep-fetch, store, and read (we already know this bug; it now sits under the dashboard headline).
11. **Ship-blocking QA rule**: every feature verified on the *rendered* artifact signed-in, which is how we caught the sticky-payload, same-day-dilution, and false-positive-pool bugs this arc. Keep that.

### E. Intuitiveness principles (apply across coach + player)
12. **One primary action per screen.** The Coaches Hub redesign (single Roster Readiness CTA) is the right pattern — extend it everywhere.
13. **Zero dead ends.** Never show an empty pool with "go author one." Offer the one-tap fix inline (install a starter Cover 1, import from week plan).
14. **Guided by default, Expert opt-in.** We built the wizard; make Guided the default for new programs.
15. **Never interrupt a rep.** Formation mapping and similar prompts go to a queue reviewed between reps, not a modal mid-test.
16. **Sideline ergonomics** — large targets, one-thumb reachability, high contrast (the ODK dark palette is aimed at this), offline-tolerant.

### F. Two-sided value (our structural advantage)
17. **Player home = assignment → why → rep → proof.** One screen: what I'm being asked to fix, why it matters this week, the reps, and my improvement.
18. **Recruiting upside stays visible to players and parents.** This is the thing SpiralXO's parent portal can't match and it drives adoption from the family side inward.

### G. Explicitly don't chase
Equipment management, generic calendar/comms parity, or a feature-for-feature answer to their 9-tool teardown. Every hour spent there is an hour not spent on the loop that actually differentiates us.

## 12. Addendum — what the 9-minute Lite walkthrough revealed

A full product demo (transcript) materially sharpens the picture. The core positioning in §6 holds, but four things get **stronger** for them and one gets **much weaker** — and that weakness is the best opening we have.

### The architecture, in their own words

Everything automates off one object: **the Play Pool.** For each call the coach enters shifts, formations, motions, tags, concepts — **plus** situation (3rd-and-medium, 3rd-and-short, red zone, goal line) and which **fronts and coverages** to use it against. Then:

- **Call sheet** = scouting report (their fronts/coverages) **matched against** play-pool situation tags → auto-filled sheet. Lock plays, regenerate, or add manually. Prints clean, including a **wrist-coach layout** (font size, columns, width, height).
- **Practice script** = add team period → set play count → AI settings → generate. Scout cards **auto-populate** where one exists matching formation + front + coverage + pressure. Prints, or **exports directly to GoRout**.
- **Scout cards** are built in the playbook creator with the coach's own terminology; the library compounds week over week.
- **Playbook builder**: import formation (auto-saved as template) → defensive structure → DL shading → coverage with **pre-shaped zones** → hide defense → draw routes (saved as templates, auto-flipping on the backside) → color-match lines to receivers → notes auto-populate → attach clips from team drive.

### What this changes in their favor

1. **The whiteboard quiz is a real football-IQ instrument, not flashcards.** The player picks a folder and a position — the coach in the demo forces **QB/"select all"** so they study *holistically* — then **draws the entire play call with a finger on a phone**, and it grades strictly ("this is going to grade you very specifically, which annoys my players"). It also covers **blocking schemes against multiple fronts**. I under-credited this in §4; it's the strongest learning primitive they have, and their coach calls Football IQ *"the biggest lever that our team can pull every single year."*
2. **The auto-generation chain is deeper than the website suggests** — call sheet *and* practice script *and* scout-card population all fall out of one data object, with GoRout export at the end of it.
3. **Retention machinery we don't have:** an in-product onboarding journey with step-by-step videos, an FAQ with video walkthroughs, and — notably — a **public feedback board where coaches submit, upvote, and watch the roadmap** ("what projects are being worked on, what's been recently added"). That's community lock-in *and* free product direction.
4. **Admin stickiness:** recurring coach responsibilities (assign the guy who brings the tablet), plus a team drive for drill books and technique manuals. Small features, high switching cost.
5. **The trial is more generous than the site implies** — the demo closes with **a full 30 days free**, versus the website's "case-by-case."

### What it reveals as their weakness — and it's significant

**Their automation is a matching engine over hand-entered metadata, not a derivation engine.** By their own admission the play pool is *"definitely a task and is going to require some upfront work on your part, but maybe a full day of work"* — and *"the quality of the call sheet is directly related to the quality of your play pool and scouting information."* So the headline feature is downstream of a **full day of manual tagging**, and it degrades quietly if the coach's tagging is thin. That's a large adoption tax hiding behind an "AI" label.

Three more, now confirmed rather than inferred:

- **Player practice is self-selected, not prescribed.** The player chooses what to quiz on. Nothing in the product says *"you are failing depth vs Cover 1 — here are six reps of exactly that."* And across nine minutes there is **no re-test, no improvement tracking, no week-over-week delta.**
- **The learning primitives are offense-shaped** — route drawing and blocking schemes. There's no defensive analog to alignment graded on relationship × leverage × depth against a composed coverage with motion adjustment.
- **Lite shares the playbook by password + link, not player accounts** — so at the free tier there's no per-player accountability at all.

### The strategic opening: *derive, don't tag*

This is the sharpest takeaway of the whole analysis. Their chain requires a coach to hand-tag situational metadata for a full day before the automation works. **We already own the machinery to derive most of it**: scouting import, `def_aligns` / week plans, `composeDefense`, and — uniquely — **rep results at cell resolution that tell us what our players actually execute.**

That means we can beat their headline feature on two axes at once:

1. **No full-day tax.** Import film/scouting → auto-derive fronts, coverages, situations, and candidate calls. The coach reviews and adjusts instead of building from zero.
2. **Better ranking than they can compute.** They rank calls by *what the coach typed in*. We can rank by *what this team actually executes well* — because we have execution data per scheme cell and they have none.

> **Positioning line:** *"They automate off what you type in. We automate off what your team actually does."*

### Revised build priorities (supersedes §9 ordering)

| Rank | Item | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Import-to-install / derive-don't-tag** (§9 #4–5) | Simultaneously kills our cold start *and* attacks their full-day play-pool tax. Biggest strategic lever. |
| 2 | **Hudl/QwikCut round-trip** (§9 #7) | Feeds #1 and removes the top objection. |
| 3 | **Prescribed practice for players** — Focus Today drives the player's rep queue, not self-selection | Their quiz is strong; our differentiator is that ours is *assigned by diagnosis and re-tested*. Make that visible. |
| 4 | **Auto-generated practice script + scout cards from week plan** | Straight feature gap; we have the pieces (week plans, practice scripts) and don't chain them. |
| 5 | **Plumbing + intuitiveness** (§9 D/E) | Still ship-blocking for credibility. |
| 6 | **Public feedback/roadmap board** | Cheap, high-retention, and it's how they're compounding coach goodwill. |
| — | *Deprioritize* | Team drive, coach task assignment, wrist-coach printing — real but low-leverage; revisit after 1–4. |

**Also worth noting:** their GoRout export means they're integrating *with* the practice-wristband ecosystem rather than competing with it. Worth deciding whether we do the same or treat GoRout as a competitor.
